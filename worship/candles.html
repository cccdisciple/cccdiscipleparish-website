Candles in Celestial Worship

Christ, Light & the Seven Spirits of God

Candles have been part of the worship of Celestial Church of Christ from the early life of the Church.

Their meaning begins with light. From the first pages of Scripture to the heavenly worship described in Revelation, light points toward the presence, truth, holiness, and glory of God.

Above all, the candle directs the worshipper toward Jesus Christ, who declared, “I am the light of the world” (John 8:12).

“Let There Be Light”

The biblical story begins in darkness until God speaks: “Let there be light” (Genesis 1:3).

Light becomes one of Scripture's great images of God's creative power and the defeat of darkness. The Gospel of John later declares concerning Christ, “The light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not” (John 1:5).

Within Celestial worship, the candle participates in this biblical language of light.

Christ — The Light of the World

The central meaning of the candle is not the wax. It is not the flame considered independently.

The candle directs the worshipper toward Christ.

“I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life.”
John 8:12

Kindling the Heart

The lighting of candles at the beginning of worship is also a call to inward preparation.

The congregation has entered the presence of God. Distraction gives way to attention. Conversation gives way to worship. The heart is turned toward God.

AS THE CANDLE IS KINDLED,
LET THE HEART ALSO BE KINDLED TOWARD GOD.

The physical act of lighting the candle reminds the worshipper to become spiritually attentive and ready for worship.

Why Seven Candles?

Seven holds a prominent place in Celestial worship.

Revelation gives the clearest biblical image: “And there were seven lamps of fire burning before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God” (Revelation 4:5).

Zechariah 4 also presents a seven-lamp stand in connection with the Spirit of God and the declaration, “Not by might, nor by power, but by my spirit, saith the LORD of hosts” (Zechariah 4:6).

The seven candles at the altar therefore direct the worshipper toward the Spirit of God and the heavenly imagery of worship before His throne.

The seven altar candles are arranged in the distinctive Celestial configuration — forming interconnected triangular relationships — rather than as a Jewish menorah. This prescribed CCC arrangement is not replaced with a menorah.

How Many Candles Are Used?

The number of candles used can vary according to the particular service or spiritual assignment.

The Church has an established liturgical order in which different services may call for different numbers of candles.

One Candle

The early morning service.

Two Candles

The Service of Songs and related funeral settings.

Three Candles

Ordinary weekday worship settings.

Four Candles

Certain intercessory or spiritual prayer assignments.

Seven Candles

Sunday and principal worship, at the principal altar arrangement.

Why White Candles?

Celestial Church of Christ uses white candles in worship. The Church's membership handbook specifically states that only white candles are used and that colored or image candles are not permitted.

The white candle participates in the larger Celestial language of light, purity, holiness, and consecration.

This also creates a visual relationship between the white Sutana and the white candle: both direct the worshipper toward purity and the life of God.

The Candle Is Not the Source

The candle has sacred meaning within worship, but the source of divine power is God.

Our faith is not in wax or flame. God is the source of power. The candle points beyond itself toward Christ, who declared, “I am the light of the world,” and accompanies the worship and prayer of the Church.

Threatening another person by saying, “I will light a candle for you,” misrepresents the proper purpose of candlelight in Celestial worship. The candle is associated with light, prayer, worship, spiritual preparation, and Christ — not personal threats or intimidation.

Order in Worship

The lighting and changing of altar candles belongs to the wardens according to the Church's order.

Candles are not changed while prayer or worship songs are in progress. Candle changes take place during Bible reading, announcements, and the thanks-offering.

Keeping the Light Burning

Scripture repeatedly uses the image of a lamp that remains burning.

Exodus 27:20–21 speaks of the lamp being kept burning. Jesus' parable of the wise and foolish virgins in Matthew 25 emphasizes readiness, watchfulness, and keeping oil for the lamps.

The candle therefore becomes a personal spiritual question:

IS YOUR OWN LIGHT STILL BURNING?

The worshipper is called to remain prayerful, faithful, spiritually awake, and ready before God.

Rekindling the Light

A flame can grow dim.

A believer can likewise experience seasons of weakness, discouragement, spiritual dryness, or distance. Celestial teaching has used the image of a previously burning candle being rekindled as a picture of spiritual restoration.

God is able to rekindle what has grown dim.

Be the Light

Jesus did not only call Himself the Light of the world. He also told His followers, “Ye are the light of the world” (Matthew 5:14).

The candle therefore becomes both a reminder of Christ and a commission to the believer.

The light received in worship is not meant to remain within the Sanctuary. The Celestian is called to carry the light of Christ into the home, workplace, community, and world.

Common Misconceptions

Select a question to read the answer.

Are CCC candles used for curses?

No. Threatening someone by saying that a candle will be lit against them misrepresents the purpose of candlelight in Celestial worship. The candle is associated with light, prayer, worship, and Christ — not with personal threats or intimidation.

Is the candle itself the source of power?

No. God is the source. The candle carries sacred biblical symbolism within prayer and worship.

Why does CCC not use many candle colors?

Celestial Church of Christ uses white candles according to its established practice.

Does prayer become impossible without a candle?

No. Prayer is directed to God. Candles belong to the liturgical and spiritual order of the Church, but God is not confined to the physical object.

The candle seen in Celestial worship is more than a flame.

It is a reminder.

Christ is the Light of the world.

As the physical candle is kindled, the worshipper is called to allow the light of God to be kindled within the heart — to leave darkness behind, remain spiritually watchful, and carry the light of Christ into the world.
