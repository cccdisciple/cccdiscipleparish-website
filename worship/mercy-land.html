Mercy Land

Ile Aanu — A Place of Mercy, Prayer, and Restoration

Mercy Land, known in Yoruba as Ile Aanu, holds an important place in the prayer life of Celestial Church of Christ. It is a consecrated place set apart for prayer, spiritual reflection, prophetic ministry, and seeking the mercy of God.

Within the early language of the Church, Mercy Land was also associated with Ìjù — the wilderness: a place separated from ordinary activity where the worshipper withdraws before God.

Mercy Land is approached in faith. The worshipper comes believing God for mercy, restoration, healing, direction, fruitfulness, deliverance, and the fulfillment of His promises.

From Ìjù — The Wilderness

Mercy Land was traditionally understood as a place set apart from the main activity of the church — a place of withdrawal, prayer, and spiritual encounter.

The image of the wilderness appears repeatedly throughout Scripture as a place where human distractions are stripped away and attention is turned toward God. Moses encountered God in the wilderness. Israel was formed there. Elijah encountered God there. Jesus Himself withdrew into wilderness places for prayer.

Within Celestial spirituality, this idea of separation remains important. Mercy Land offers a place where the worshipper may withdraw from ordinary activity and seek God with greater focus.

“Where It Is Lost, There It Shall Be Found”

“WHERE IT IS LOST, THERE IT SHALL BE FOUND.”

This principle calls the worshipper to approach Mercy Land with faith, believing God for mercy and restoration.

Whatever has been lost in one's journey — peace, opportunity, direction, fruitfulness, strength, hope, or another area of life — Mercy Land is a place to bring that need before God in prayer and believe Him for restoration.

Its spiritual meaning is illustrated through two gardens.

The Garden of Eden — The First Adam

The biblical story of humanity begins in a garden.

God planted the Garden of Eden, and humanity lived in fellowship with Him. Through the disobedience of the first Adam, however, sin and death entered the human experience.

The garden therefore becomes associated not only with humanity's beginning, but also with what was lost through the Fall.

Romans 5 contrasts Adam's disobedience with the obedience of Jesus Christ, while 1 Corinthians 15 presents Christ as the Last Adam through whom life comes (Romans 5:12–19; 1 Corinthians 15:21–22, 45–49).

“For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive.”
1 Corinthians 15:22

The Garden of Gethsemane — Christ, the Last Adam

The story of redemption also brings us to a garden.

In Gethsemane, Jesus Christ submitted Himself completely to the will of the Father:

“Not my will, but thine, be done.”
Luke 22:42

From Gethsemane He proceeded toward the Cross, where He gave Himself for the redemption of humanity.

Celestial teaching surrounding Mercy Land therefore draws a spiritual connection between Eden and Gethsemane:

In Eden, the first Adam disobeyed.

In Gethsemane, Christ submitted Himself to the Father.

Through the first Adam came sin and death.

Through Christ came redemption and life.

What humanity lost through Adam is restored through Christ.

This is the faith reflected in the Mercy Land principle:

“Where it is lost, there it shall be found.”

A Garden of Prayer

Mercy Land is commonly arranged as a garden-like environment.

Trees, plants, flowers, sand, water, and the seven-candle altar are not simply decorative features. Together they help form a place intentionally separated for prayer.

The garden setting recalls Creation, Eden, Gethsemane, spiritual retreat, growth, fruitfulness, and renewed fellowship with God.

Mercy Land may therefore be used for personal prayer, spiritual retreat, prophetic ministry, and spiritual assignments performed according to the order of the Church and the direction of the Holy Spirit.

Why Is There Sand?

Sand is one of the most recognizable features of Mercy Land.

Its presence is closely connected to the history of the Church and the Pastor-Founder's instruction concerning spiritual work previously taken to the seaside.

In his April 1974 address, Pastor-Founder S.B.J. Oshoffa instructed Celestians not to go to the seaside or beach for spiritual “jobs.” He stated that Celestial Church of Christ had been blessed with Mercy Land, and directed that work which might previously have been taken to the beach should instead be carried out there.

The sand within Mercy Land therefore preserves an important connection with the seaside while keeping the spiritual work within the place God provided for the Church.

Abraham's promised descendants are also compared in Scripture to the sand upon the seashore (Genesis 22:17).

Why Mercy Land Instead of the Beach or Prayer Mountain?

Mercy Land is not simply one prayer location among many.

Pastor-Founder S.B.J. Oshoffa specifically directed Celestians away from taking spiritual work to beaches and seaside locations and established Mercy Land as the place for such work within the Church.

This instruction helps preserve the spiritual identity and order given to Celestial Church of Christ rather than importing practices from elsewhere.

The beach may have ordinary uses, and CCC baptism has historically involved natural bodies of water. The prohibition concerns taking spiritual work or prayer assignments to the seaside as a substitute for Mercy Land.

Gethsemane and Spiritual Retreat

Before His arrest, Jesus withdrew with His disciples to Gethsemane and prayed intensely before the Father.

Mercy Land likewise provides a place to withdraw, pray, seek direction, and surrender a matter before God.

Not every prayer requires Mercy Land, but Mercy Land gives the Church a dedicated environment where the worshipper can intentionally step away from ordinary activity and seek the face of God.

Good Friday and Mercy Land

Mercy Land also carries particular significance during the Passion season.

Official CCC practice has included Good Friday observances at Mercy Land.

The connection is especially meaningful because of Mercy Land's spiritual association with Gethsemane — the place where Christ prayed before proceeding toward His suffering and crucifixion.

Prophetic Ministry

Mercy Land also provides an appropriate setting for prophetic ministry.

Within Celestial Church of Christ, prophetic revelation is expected to operate within order. Prophecy should not unnecessarily disrupt the established order of worship.

When a message requires attention outside the immediate flow of a service, Mercy Land may serve as one of the places where prophetic ministry continues.

Special Services at Mercy Land

The Church's Order of Service includes specific occasions connected with Mercy Land.

These include a Friday service for Prophets, Prophetesses, Dreamers, and Visioners, as well as the Friday prayer service associated with pregnant women.

These services demonstrate that Mercy Land is not simply an optional garden or architectural feature. It belongs to the established prayer and spiritual life of Celestial Church of Christ.

The Seven-Candle Altar

A seven-candle altar is traditionally associated with Mercy Land.

The number seven holds a prominent place throughout Celestial worship and is connected with spiritual completeness and the seven Spirits of God.

“Seven lamps of fire burning before the throne, which are the seven Spirits of God.”
Revelation 4:5

The seven lights therefore direct the worshipper toward the Spirit of God and the heavenly imagery that appears throughout Celestial worship.

Learn About Candles in Celestial Worship

Water — The Water of Life

Water holds a significant place throughout Scripture.

At Creation, the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters. Water sustains physical life. It appears throughout Scripture in cleansing, healing, deliverance, renewal, and salvation.

“The water that I shall give him shall be in him a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”
John 4:14

He later cried, “If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink” (John 7:37). John explains that He spoke concerning the Spirit.

For this reason, the presence of water within Mercy Land carries deep spiritual meaning. A well, fountain, or water feature recalls life, cleansing, renewal, the Holy Spirit, and Christ as the source of Living Water.

Facing East

Mercy Land traditionally faces East.

“And the LORD God planted a garden eastward in Eden” (Genesis 2:8).

Within Mercy Land, the eastward orientation reinforces the garden imagery and recalls humanity's beginning in Eden.

Trees, Plants & Flowers

Trees and plants strengthen the garden character of Mercy Land.

They recall Creation, life, growth, fruitfulness, shelter, and the Garden of Eden.

No single species of tree should be presented as universally required unless supported by authoritative CCC documentation.

The significance lies in Mercy Land retaining the character of a living garden set apart for prayer.

Sacred Ground & Proper Attire

Mercy Land is consecrated ground and is approached with reverence.

CCC teaching concerning sacred ground is closely connected with God's command to Moses: “Put off thy shoes from off thy feet, for the place whereon thou standest is holy ground” (Exodus 3:5). Joshua received the same command (Joshua 5:15).

Visitors should follow the established order of the parish concerning proper worship attire and entry into Mercy Land.

The garden reminds us of where humanity began.

The Fall reminds us of what humanity lost.

Gethsemane reminds us of Christ's obedience.

The Cross reminds us of redemption.

The Resurrection reminds us of restored life.

And Mercy Land calls the worshipper back to God — believing in His mercy, His promises, and His restoring power.

WHERE IT IS LOST,
THERE IT SHALL BE FOUND.
