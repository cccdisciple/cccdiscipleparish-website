The Sutana

The White Garment of Celestial Worship

One of the first things a visitor notices in Celestial Church of Christ is the sea of white garments.

The white prayer and worship garment worn by Celestians is called the Sutana — pronounced SU-TA-NA.

It is more than a church uniform. The Sutana continually reminds the worshipper of holiness, purity, humility, consecration, Christian witness, and the heavenly calling of the believer.

What Is the Sutana?

The Sutana is the prescribed white prayer and worship garment of Celestial Church of Christ.

It covers the body modestly and is worn according to the established order of the Church.

Different forms of Sutana may identify distinctions in sex, ministry, prophetic calling, anointment, or rank, but white remains the defining color of the garment.

The Sutana is set apart for worship and should be treated accordingly.

Why White?

The biblical imagery of white garments is especially powerful.

David prayed, “Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow” (Psalm 51:7). At the Transfiguration, Christ's garments became exceedingly white (Mark 9:2–3). In Revelation, those who belong to God repeatedly appear before Him clothed in white.

“They shall walk with me in white: for they are worthy.”
Revelation 3:4

John later sees a great multitude “clothed with white robes” (Revelation 7:9). And Revelation 19 describes fine linen, clean and bright, associated with the saints (Revelation 19:7–8).

For the Celestian, the white Sutana therefore becomes a visible reminder of cleansing, holiness, righteousness, faithfulness, and a life set apart for God.

The Garment of the Saints

The Sutana reminds the worshipper not only of how we come before God today, but of whom we are called to become before Him eternally.

The recurring image of white garments in Revelation points toward the redeemed people of God standing in His presence.

Putting on the Sutana should therefore direct the mind beyond clothing itself and toward the believer's heavenly calling.

“Do Not Defile Your Garment”

Revelation 3 speaks of believers who had “not defiled their garments” (Revelation 3:4–5).

This gives the white garment a moral as well as a visual significance.

Keeping a Sutana physically white is important, but the deeper call is to keep one's life before God. The garment reminds the Celestian to pursue holiness, integrity, faithfulness, humility, obedience, purity of conduct, and Christian love.

The whiteness of the garment should be reflected in the life of the person wearing it.

A Sacred Garment

A Sutana is not treated as an ordinary white gown.

Within CCC practice, a new Sutana is sanctified before being placed into regular sacred use.

This reflects a larger biblical principle: what is dedicated for the worship of God is treated with reverence.

The Sutana should therefore be kept clean, worn properly, and used in a manner consistent with its sacred purpose.

Covering Our Nakedness

The account below is preserved within Celestial Church of Christ history as a historical account of the Pastor-Founder, not as a passage of Scripture.

CCC history preserves an account from Lagos Bar Beach in 1954 during an anointment gathering attended by Pastor-Founder S.B.J. Oshoffa and members of the Church.

Within the Pastor-Founder's account, a woman present in ordinary dress was questioned by Christ concerning why she had come “naked.” Those around her understood the statement in relation to her not being dressed in Sutana like the other members.

Within Celestial teaching, this account helped reinforce the Sutana as the proper sacred garment in which members present themselves for worship.

The Sutana & Barefoot Worship

The prescribed prayer and worship robe of Celestial Church of Christ is called the Sutana. The Church's membership handbook identifies the Sutana, the loin, and barefoot worship as part of the divinely received order of the Church.

Barefoot worship applies within the Sanctuary, and the Church's teaching further establishes that once the Sutana is worn, shoes are not worn with it. This means the practice is broader than simply removing shoes after entering the Sanctuary.

The Church's teaching concerning holy ground is rooted in passages such as Exodus 3:5 and Joshua 5:15.

In climates or circumstances where members cannot reasonably travel barefoot, members may arrive in ordinary clothing and footwear, change into Sutana at church, remove their shoes, worship barefoot, and dress again before leaving. Members are not expected to travel through public streets barefoot.

Modesty, Humility & Simplicity

The Sutana should direct attention toward God rather than toward personal status.

There is nothing wrong with appearing beautiful and well presented before God. Harvests and other celebrations often bring especially beautiful Sutanas.

But beauty must remain joined to humility. The question is not simply, “How impressive is my garment?” The better question is, “Whom have I come to worship?”

The Sutana is not intended to become a tool of competition, pride, intimidation, or oppression.

Beauty Within the Prescribed Form

CCC teaching emphasizes that the Sutana should retain its prescribed form rather than becoming an unrestricted fashion garment.

Transparent or excessively tight Sutanas are not permitted. The garment should preserve modesty, reverence, and the prescribed sacred form of Celestial worship. The Church's handbook includes guidance concerning proper construction and prohibits unauthorized decorative alterations.

Beauty in a Sutana is not forbidden, but beauty should remain within the prescribed form of the sacred garment. The Sutana should not become an unrestricted fashion garment or a means of competition, pride, or intimidation.

The principle is not that the Sutana must be unattractive. Rather: beauty should remain within the sacred form given to the Church.

Leadership & Ranks

The Four-Corner Sutana & Prophetic Ministry

The four-corner Sutana is particularly associated with the prophetic line.

But the garment itself does not make someone a Prophet or Prophetess. A prophetic calling is not self-declared simply by changing clothing.

Prophetic ministry within CCC involves spiritual manifestation, discernment, testing, order, and recognition within the Church.

Learn About Prophetic Ministry

Rank Is Responsibility

A more advanced or elaborate Sutana should never be interpreted as meaning that one Christian has greater human worth than another.

Within the Church, advancement in anointment and responsibility should reflect service, spiritual maturity, and greater responsibility toward God and His people.

THE GARMENT DOES NOT EXALT THE WEARER.
IT REMINDS THE WEARER OF GREATER RESPONSIBILITY.

The Sutana as Christian Witness

A uniform makes a person immediately recognizable. In the same way, the Sutana makes the Celestian visible.

Because of this, conduct while wearing it matters. People may know nothing about a particular member personally, yet recognize that person as belonging to Celestial Church of Christ. The believer therefore carries a responsibility to represent Christ and the Church well.

Jesus said, “Ye are the light of the world” (Matthew 5:14–16). Paul also described believers as an epistle “known and read of all men” (2 Corinthians 3:2–3).

When a Celestian puts on the Sutana, people are reading the life alongside the garment.

The Sutana is white, but its message reaches beyond color.

It calls the worshipper toward holiness.

It teaches humility.

It reminds the believer of heaven.

It identifies the Celestian before the world.

And each time it is worn, it calls the wearer to live in a way worthy of the Christ before whom we have come to worship.
