Water in Celestial Church of Christ

Life, Cleansing, Renewal & the Living Water

Water appears everywhere in the story of Scripture.

It is present at Creation. It sustains life. It appears in judgment and deliverance, cleansing and healing, baptism and new birth. And in the words of Jesus Christ, water becomes one of the Bible's great images of eternal life and the Holy Spirit.

For this reason, water holds an important place throughout the worship and spiritual life of Celestial Church of Christ.

Water at Creation

The Bible introduces water immediately: “And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters” (Genesis 1:2).

From the beginning, Scripture places water alongside the activity of God in Creation. The passage does not teach that water is God. God is the Creator.

Water belongs to His creation and becomes one of the great biblical images through which Scripture communicates life, cleansing, judgment, salvation, renewal, and the work of the Spirit.

Water & Life

Physical life depends upon water. Plants, animals, and human beings cannot survive without it.

Scripture repeatedly takes this physical reality and uses it to communicate spiritual truth. Just as the body thirsts for water, the human soul needs the life that comes from God.

This prepares us to understand why Jesus later describes what He gives as Living Water.

Judgment & Salvation — The Flood

In Genesis 6–9, the waters of the Flood become associated with both judgment and preservation.

A world characterized by violence and wickedness comes under judgment, while Noah and his household are preserved because Noah responds obediently to God's instruction.

The lesson is not that water possesses independent power. God is the One who judges, saves, commands, and preserves.

Judgment & Deliverance — The Red Sea

Exodus 14 presents a similar pattern. The sea opens before Israel and becomes a pathway of deliverance. The same sea becomes judgment upon Pharaoh's pursuing army.

For Israel

Water → Deliverance

For Pharaoh's Army

Water → Judgment

Paul later uses Israel's passage through the sea in baptismal imagery (1 Corinthians 10:1–2).

Naaman — Cleansing, Healing & Obedience

Naaman came to Elisha seeking healing from leprosy. Elisha instructed him to wash in the Jordan seven times (2 Kings 5).

Naaman initially resisted because the instruction appeared too simple and the Jordan seemed unimpressive to him. But when he humbled himself and obeyed, he was healed.

The story brings together several important themes: obedience, humility, cleansing, healing, and transformation.

Naaman's washing may also remind Christian readers of baptism, though his washing in the Jordan should not be presented as though it literally were Christian baptism.

Baptism — Water & New Life

Water becomes especially significant within Christian baptism.

Jesus taught Nicodemus about being born of water and the Spirit (John 3:5). The New Testament presents baptism in connection with entering a new life in Christ — dying to the old and rising into the new.

Jesus Himself was baptized in the Jordan. When He came up from the water, the heavens opened, the Spirit descended like a dove, and the Father declared His pleasure in the Son (Matthew 3:13–17).

Jesus did not need cleansing from sin. His baptism fulfills righteousness and becomes part of the revelation of His identity and mission.

Water & Healing

John 5 tells the story of a man who had suffered for thirty-eight years near the Pool of Bethesda.

His attention had long been fixed upon reaching the water. Then Jesus came to him. The man who could not reach the pool found the true source of healing standing before him. Christ commanded him to rise, take up his bed, and walk (John 5:1–9).

Christ — The Living Water

At Jacob's well, Jesus told the Samaritan woman, “Whosoever drinketh of the water that I shall give him shall never thirst” (John 4:14).

He explained that the water He gives becomes “a well of water springing up into everlasting life.”

“If any man thirst, let him come unto me, and drink.”
John 7:37–39

John explains that Jesus spoke concerning the Spirit. This gives us the central Christian meaning behind water:

THE DEEPEST THIRST OF HUMANITY
IS ANSWERED IN CHRIST.

Blood & Water from Christ

At the Crucifixion, a soldier pierced the side of Jesus: “And forthwith came there out blood and water” (John 19:34).

The image has held profound significance throughout Christian reflection. Within CCC hymnody, the blood and water flowing from Christ's pierced side are also remembered.

Water Turned into Wine

At Cana, Jesus performed His first recorded sign by transforming water into wine (John 2:1–11).

The sign reveals His glory and demonstrates His authority over creation.

Celestians may also reflect devotionally upon the event as a reminder that Christ must remain central in the affairs of life.

Water in CCC Worship

Water appears repeatedly within Celestial worship and spiritual life.

It may be encountered in connection with sanctification, baptism, prayer, spiritual baths, healing, deliverance, the Mercy Land, altar water, prophetic assignments, the Naming Ceremony, and services in which water forms part of the prescribed worship materials.

These practices make greater sense when viewed against the biblical language already explored: life, cleansing, renewal, healing, salvation, the Holy Spirit, and Christ the Living Water.

Holy / Sanctified Water

Within CCC, water may be prayed over and sanctified for spiritual use.

This should not be made unnecessarily mysterious. Sanctification means that something is set apart through prayer for sacred use. The faith of the worshipper remains directed toward God.

Water & Spiritual Baths

Spiritual baths form part of the prayer life of CCC when directed for a particular purpose.

The water itself is not separated from prayer. The spiritual instruction, the Word of God, prayer, and faith form the context in which the assignment is carried out.

Altar Water

Altar water is not limited to folk sayings such as using it only for someone with a temper.

Its prayerful use may vary according to the particular spiritual need, prayer, and direction involved. Its significance belongs to the wider biblical and Celestial understanding of sanctified water.

Different Forms of Water

CCC spiritual assignments may involve ordinary water, river water, seawater, rainwater, dew, coconut water, and other water specified through spiritual instruction.

Different forms of water do not operate according to a simplistic fixed chart. It would be inaccurate to teach that rainwater always means one thing, seawater always means another, and dew always serves one fixed purpose. The particular water used depends upon the spiritual instruction and the purpose of the prayer or assignment.

Flowing Water

For certain spiritual assignments involving natural bodies of water, flowing water is preferred to stagnant water. Flowing water carries the imagery of movement and continuing life, while stagnant water may represent stagnation. This is spiritual symbolism within CCC practice, not a scientific claim.

Dew

Dew may sometimes be specified in a spiritual assignment. Daniel 4 describes Nebuchadnezzar during his period of humbling as being wet with the dew of heaven (Daniel 4). CCC teaching draws spiritual meaning from this imagery in certain assignments involving restoration and deliverance — the biblical account centers upon God's judgment, Nebuchadnezzar's humbling, and his eventual acknowledgment of the Most High; God, not the dew itself, remains the source.

Water in Special Services

Water forms part of the prescribed worship materials in several Celestial services, specifically the Seeker Service, the New Moon Service, and the service for Prophets and Prophetesses.

Its presence repeatedly returns the worshipper to the themes of life, cleansing, the Spirit of God, and Christ the Living Water. For the Seeker Service: the candle points to Christ, the divine light; the water points to Christ, the source of life and Living Water; the fruits point to increase, multiplication, fruitfulness, and blessing.

Water, Beaches & the Mercy Land

A spiritual instruction requiring seawater or river water does not necessarily require the person to perform the entire spiritual work at the beach or river.

The water may be collected and brought to the Church or an appropriate place of prayer according to the Church's proper order. Pastor-Founder S.B.J. Oshoffa specifically corrected the practice of taking spiritual “jobs” to the beach and directed Celestians toward the Mercy Land.

Learn About the Mercy Land

Water appears throughout Scripture from Creation to the teaching of Christ.

It carries images of life, cleansing, judgment, salvation, healing, renewal, baptism, and the Holy Spirit. For this reason, water continues to hold an important place within the worship and spiritual life of Celestial Church of Christ.

Yet the deepest meaning points beyond physical water. Jesus Christ remains the source.

“IF ANY MAN THIRST,
LET HIM COME UNTO ME, AND DRINK.”
