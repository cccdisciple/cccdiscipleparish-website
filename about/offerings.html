Offerings & Giving

Giving is worship before it is a transaction.

Giving in Celestial Church of Christ is an act of worship, gratitude, obedience, faith, and care for others. Scripture presents several expressions of giving, and within the Church these are not all understood in exactly the same way. Learning the distinction helps us give with understanding rather than treating every gift as the same thing.

This page is educational. To give to Disciple Parish, visit our giving page.

Interactive Giving Guide

These terms are all related to giving — but they do not all mean the same thing. Select any form of giving below to learn what it means, its Scripture, and its place in worship.

Offering Worship

An offering is a voluntary gift brought before God as an expression of worship, gratitude, devotion, and willingness to support His work.

Giving appears throughout Scripture as part of the worshipper's response to God. The emphasis is not simply on transferring money, but on the heart with which the gift is brought.

“God loveth a cheerful giver.” 2 Corinthians 9:7

Within worship, an offering may be received as part of the congregation's giving. It should not automatically be treated as identical to the Tithe, Thanks Offering, Seed Offering, or every other form of giving simply because money is involved.

Key Distinction

Offering concerns voluntarily bringing a gift before God in worship. Do not add a fixed percentage. Do not call ordinary Offering a Tithe.

Tithe The Tenth

The Tithe is the giving of a tenth.

The principle appears throughout Scripture. Abraham gave Melchizedek a tenth, the Law speaks of the tithe as belonging unto the Lord, and Malachi calls God's people to bring the tithes into the storehouse.

Scripture

Genesis 14:18–20
Abraham gave Melchizedek a tenth.
Leviticus 27:30
The tithe is described as holy unto the Lord.
Malachi 3:10
God's people are instructed to bring the tithes into the storehouse.

In CCC Worship

Tithe has a distinct place within the giving life of the Church and should not simply be collapsed into the general Offering.

At Disciple Parish, the tithe box is known as the Treasury of Heaven. It is carried reverently as part of the service.

Prayer over the Tithe is offered by the Shepherd or a person delegated by the Shepherd.

Elders and Wardens may assist with the practical receiving and handling of the congregation's giving according to the order of the service.

Key Distinction

Tithe concerns the tenth. Do not describe every gift to the Church as Tithe.

Thanks Offering Gratitude

Thanks Offering — Ope

Thanks Offering is giving brought before God as an expression of gratitude and praise.

It gives the worshipper an opportunity to respond to God's goodness by giving thanks.

Thanks Offering may be connected with gratitude for God's help, protection, provision, answered prayer, deliverance, milestones, or other reasons for which a person, family, or group desires to thank God. These are examples, not a checklist restricting when thanks may be offered.

Scripture

Psalm 50:14 · Psalm 116:17

In CCC Worship

Thanks Offering has a visible place in Celestial worship.

At Disciple Parish, those bringing Thanks Offering may come forward from the Sanctuary entrance with singing and dancing as the offering is brought before God.

The movement, music, and joy express the character of thanksgiving: the worshipper is not merely giving something, but openly giving thanks to God.

Key Distinction

Thanks Offering concerns gratitude. Do not reduce it to an ordinary collection simply because money or gifts may be presented.

Freewill / Collections Willing Giving

A Freewill Offering is giving that comes willingly from the worshipper.

CCC teaching has historically spoken of Freewill Offering, popularly known as Collections, alongside Tithes, Thanks Offering, and Alms. The emphasis is willingness rather than compulsion.

Scripture

Exodus 35:29 · 2 Corinthians 9:7

In Worship

Collections may be received during worship for the work and needs of the Church. Depending upon the service and purpose, more than one collection may be received.

Key Distinction

Freewill giving emphasizes willingness. Do not present every Collection as a Tithe. Do not imply that every designated collection constitutes a completely separate theological category of giving.

Seed Offering Faith

A Seed Offering is giving made in faith, often connected with prayer, a purpose, a need, or an expectation placed before God.

The biblical imagery of sowing and reaping helps Christians understand generosity, faith, and the disposition of the giver.

Scripture

2 Corinthians 9:6–8

Important Clarification

A Seed Offering is not the purchase of a miracle, blessing, prophecy, healing, or answer to prayer. God's grace cannot be bought.

The meaning of the seed is found in giving faithfully before God, not in treating the offering as a financial transaction that forces God to produce a particular result.

Key Distinction

Seed Offering emphasizes giving in faith.

First Fruits First of New Increase

First Fruits expresses the principle of honoring God first from new increase and provision.

In Scripture, the first fruits were brought before God in recognition that the harvest and provision ultimately came from Him.

Scripture

Exodus 23:19 · Deuteronomy 26:1–11 · Proverbs 3:9–10

Understanding the Principle Today

In contemporary Christian and CCC practice, First Fruits may be associated with the beginning of a new season of increase — for example, new-year earnings, a new job, a new business, or another significant beginning in provision.

The underlying principle is: honor God first from new increase.

Key Distinction

Tithe concerns the tenth. First Fruits concerns honoring God first from new increase. They should not be presented as the same thing.

Alms Helping Those in Need

Alms is giving directed toward helping people in need.

This form of giving reminds the Church that generosity is not limited to what is placed into a collection during worship.

Christ teaches that giving to those in need should not be performed merely to receive praise from others.

Scripture

Matthew 6:1–4 · Acts 10:4 · 1 John 3:17–18

Key Distinction

Alms concerns care for people in need. Do not automatically describe money placed into the ordinary church collection as Alms.

How Giving Appears in Sunday Worship

Giving is woven into the order of worship rather than treated as a separate commercial transaction.

  1. Sermon
  2. ↓
  3. Collections / Giving
  4. ↓
  5. Tithe
  6. ↓
  7. Thanks Offering

After the sermon, the service may include collections and the receiving of Tithe.

The Tithe is treated distinctly. At Disciple Parish, the tithe box is called the Treasury of Heaven and is carried reverently. Prayer over the Tithe is offered by the Shepherd or someone delegated by the Shepherd.

Elders and Wardens assist with the practical order of receiving the congregation's giving.

Thanks Offering has its own expression. Those bringing Thanks Offering may come forward from the Sanctuary entrance with singing and dancing as their thanks is presented before God.

The purpose of these distinctions is not to make giving complicated. They help worshippers understand why they are giving and what their gift expresses before God.

Designated Giving

Not every named fund or collection represents a separate theological form of offering.

A church may receive giving designated for particular needs or ministries, such as:

  • building and maintenance
  • welfare
  • ministry work
  • media
  • special projects
  • other approved church needs

The designation explains where the gift is intended to be used. It does not necessarily create an entirely new biblical category of giving.

The Heart of Giving

Christian giving should never be reduced to pressure, fear, competition, or the idea that God's favor can be purchased.

Scripture repeatedly returns attention to the heart of the giver.

“Every man according as he purposeth in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loveth a cheerful giver.”

2 Corinthians 9:7
  • Give with understanding.
  • Give willingly.
  • Give faithfully.
  • Give thankfully.
  • Care for those in need.
  • Do not treat giving as a way to purchase God's grace.

Giving in CCC Teaching

Celestial Church of Christ has long distinguished between different expressions of Christian giving.

In a 1987 lecture on the tenets of Celestial Church of Christ, Rev. Alexander Abiodun Bada identified Thanks Offering and Tithes as principal religious obligations and also identified Alms and Freewill Offering, popularly known as Collections, as important forms of giving.

This historical teaching helps explain why CCC does not need to treat every gift simply as one generic “offering.”
